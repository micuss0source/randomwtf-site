Boiled Peanuts, the Way We Actually Do It

Osiyo — from the Random What Tha Farm kitchen

Every summer somebody asks for the "recipe" like there's a secret ratio written down somewhere. There isn't. This is just how it's been done around here for as long as I can remember, and it works every time.

What you need:
  • Raw green peanuts, in the shell — about 2 lbs
  • Water, enough to cover them by a couple inches
  • Salt — roughly 1 cup per gallon of water, then adjust
  • A big stock pot and time

Rinse the peanuts good first, there's usually field dirt on them. Get them in the pot, cover with water, and stir in the salt. Don't be shy with it — under-salted boiled peanuts are the most disappointing thing you can hand somebody.

Bring it to a boil, then knock it back to a strong simmer. This is the part people mess up by rushing. Green peanuts usually take 60 to 90 minutes. Old, drier peanuts can take 3 to 4 hours and need more salt in the water to make up for it.

Forget the clock. Fish one out, crack it, taste it. You're looking for soft all the way through, not mushy, and salty enough that you can taste it in the shell water on your fingers after. If it's not there yet, put it back and give it more time.

Once they're right, kill the heat and let them sit in that salty water for at least 30 minutes before you drain them. They keep soaking up flavor the whole time they're cooling, and rushing that step is the second most common mistake after not salting enough.
