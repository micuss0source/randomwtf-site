Why We Still Hand-Pick Every Tomato

Osiyo — a little Random What Tha Farm life

Somebody asked me the other day why we don't just get a machine to speed up picking season. Fair question. The honest answer is that a machine can't tell the difference between a tomato that's ready today and one that'll be ready Thursday.

Walking the rows, you get to know what "ready" actually looks like — not just the color, but how it sits in your hand, how easy it lets go of the vine when it's really done. Pull one too early and it never quite gets there. Leave one too long and the birds beat you to it.

It's slower. There's no getting around that. But slower means every tomato that ends up at the market got looked at by an actual person who decided it was worth putting in the bin. That's not a sales pitch, it's just what happens when you do it by hand.

It also means picking season is when you notice everything else going on in the field — where the drainage is off, which rows are struggling, where something's been getting into the plants at night. You miss all of that riding a machine down the row.

So yeah, it takes longer. We're alright with that.
